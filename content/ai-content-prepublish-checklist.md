# AI content pre-publish checklist

A gate for any AI-assisted blog post, landing page, or location page before it goes live. It's built to avoid a pattern Lily Ray documented: fast traffic gains from scaled AI content, followed by a steep drop once Google treats the pages as unoriginal.

**Source:** Lily Ray, "It Works Until It Doesn't: AI Content Strategies That Backfire," Search Engine Journal. https://www.searchenginejournal.com/it-works-until-it-doesnt-ai-content-strategies-that-backfire/574820/

## Gate 1: The four questions (all must be yes)

- [ ] **Reader need.** Does this page exist because readers need it, not only because a search engine or AI tool might cite it?
- [ ] **URL-list test.** Would you be comfortable if Google, or your client, saw every URL in this content set side by side?
- [ ] **Honest bias.** If the piece favors you or a client, is that bias disclosed rather than presented as neutral?
- [ ] **Information gain.** Does it include first-party data, original insight, or experience that isn't already in the top 10 results? If a competitor could publish nearly the same page tomorrow from the same prompt, it fails.

## Gate 2: Not a high-risk format

- [ ] Not a product-vs-product comparison page built at scale.
- [ ] Not a thin "What is X" glossary page built to be extracted.
- [ ] Not a "Best [X] for [Y]" affiliate-style listicle.
- [ ] Not a listicle that ranks you or a client first without real evidence.
- [ ] Not a competitor-alternatives page. Use an evaluation framework instead.
- [ ] Not a programmatic location or language page with no unique local content.
- [ ] Not a one-question-per-page FAQ farm.
- [ ] Not off-topic content unrelated to the business.

## Gate 3: A human expert in the loop

- [ ] A person with real expertise reviewed and revised the draft. AI helped with research, briefs, organization, or synthesizing your own data, and a person decided what got published.
- [ ] The piece reflects real experience, not rephrased competitor content.
- [ ] Claims, statistics, and dates are fact-checked, and named sources are cited accurately. Google's guidance on generative AI content says it's critical to manually fact-check and review AI-generated content before publishing: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content
- [ ] The main content shows the attributes Google's quality raters look at: effort, originality, talent or skill, and accuracy. https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content
- [ ] It passes a logo-swap test: a competitor couldn't publish it unchanged by replacing the brand name.

## Gate 4: Useful to cite, without citation theater

- [ ] The page answers its main question early.
- [ ] Headings use real reader questions where that helps navigation, not as a mechanical rule.
- [ ] Important claims use specific names, dates, places, products, and numbers instead of vague nouns.
- [ ] Facts come with analysis, tradeoffs, or implications rather than hype.
- [ ] It's clear enough for a business reader and specific enough for a skeptical expert.
- [ ] Nobody is optimizing toward a rigid density target, readability grade, heading quota, or paragraph-position rule.
- [ ] The structure improves the page for people first. Google says there's no special writing style, chunk size, or schema required for its AI features: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide

## Gate 5: Writing and site checks

- [ ] No AI copy patterns. Run [banned-ai-copy-patterns.md](../copy/banned-ai-copy-patterns.md) or the `ai-copy-tells` skill.
- [ ] Canonical tag points to the right URL.
- [ ] Internal links are in place, and the page doesn't compete with an existing page for the same query.

## Gate 6: Pacing

- [ ] You considered refreshing an existing page before creating a new URL.
- [ ] New posts are paced, not published in large batches that look like scaled content.
- [ ] If the site has indexing problems, those are understood before adding more URLs.

## If a page fails

- **Gate 1 or 2:** don't publish. Find an angle with real information gain, or drop it.
- **Gate 3:** send it back for expert review.
- **Gate 4 or 5:** fix it before publishing; these are usually editing fixes.
- **Gate 6:** queue it rather than forcing it out.
