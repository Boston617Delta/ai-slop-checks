# Diagnostic patterns

These are review heuristics, not a detector. The research supports convergence and the need for context; this expanded repair menu is our practical synthesis. Repetition becomes a concern when it flattens meaning, fits many unrelated briefs equally, or interferes with use. Keep a pattern when it serves the approved design.

## Visual vocabulary

| Pattern to inspect | When it weakens the page | Specific repair | Legitimate use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Purple/blue gradients, glowing blobs, grid backgrounds | Decoration has no connection to the subject and competes with content | Establish palette roles; replace distracting background effects with subject-relevant imagery or a quiet surface | An approved brand or a visualization that benefits from gradients |
| Cream, display serif, muted earthy accent | Applied as the automatic “human” alternative across unrelated projects | Check the reference and subject; build identity through content, composition, and assets | An established editorial or lifestyle identity |
| Dark canvas plus neon accent | Suggests a technical product regardless of the actual offer | Choose surfaces for the content and audience; check muted text in the final render | A deliberate dark brand or appropriate product environment |
| Newspaper styling, tiny metadata, dense rules | Creates a publishing aesthetic with no editorial substance | Give research, articles, and hierarchy a real role; simplify decorative metadata | A publication with strong reading priorities |
| Same font treatment everywhere | Headlines, labels, prices, and body text lack distinct roles | Define a type hierarchy; tune weight, width, line height, and wrapping before adding families | One readable family with well-defined roles |
| Gradient or italic emphasis on an arbitrary headline fragment | Styling does not reinforce the message | Test the full headline unaccented; emphasize only meaningfully | Deliberate emphasis in the approved identity |
| All-caps eyebrows above every heading | Labels repeat the heading or add no orientation | Keep only labels that identify content type, category, or context | Useful navigation or editorial classification |
| Identical radius, border, and shadow across components | Unrelated elements appear equally important | Define component roles; remove containers where a list, table, or open layout communicates better | Comparable objects that need stable grouping |
| Everything is a pill | Social links, navigation, filters, and primary actions blur together | Separate primary action, navigation, and state selection visually | Compact selectable filters or an established button style |
| Decorative icons or emoji on every item | Adds noise without improving recognition | Keep icons that clarify meaning; use a consistent library and accessible labels | Repeated controls with recognizable actions |

## Structure and substance

| Pattern to inspect | When it weakens the page | Specific repair | Legitimate use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Centered badge, large headline, two buttons in every hero | The first screen fails to show what is distinctive or useful | Choose the opening based on the subject: actual work, offer, evidence, person, or product task | A clear campaign or product introduction |
| Repeated three-card sections | Content is forced into equal boxes and a fixed count | Group by visitor questions and actual relationships; vary presentation where content requires it | Three genuinely comparable choices |
| Open-ended ruled column grids: internal vertical dividers, often a top rule, no bottom rule or outer side borders | Repeated across fact strips, services, or numbered processes as a default editorial treatment; assess the open border geometry even when the grouping makes sense | Avoid this as a default. Use a plain fact line, an image with supporting facts, or a process list/timeline with spacing suited to the content. Do not automatically add a full box around the same grid | Preserve only when a specific approved reference or deliberate project decision calls for this exact treatment; useful sequencing alone does not settle the styling choice |
| Bento layout for prose | Fragmentation obscures sequence and relationships | Use continuous reading for arguments; reserve grids for independent items | Browsing independent products, projects, or tools |
| Large number tiles with vague labels | Scope, timeframe, and denominators are missing or incompatible | Add source context and units; present a chart or annotated finding where appropriate | Verifiable, comparable metrics |
| Same section sequence for every business | The template includes irrelevant features or buries essential answers | Order around buyer questions, evidence, and next steps; remove sections without a job | A tested sequence that fits the audience |
| Generic “premium” imagery | The assets could describe any company in the category | Use actual people, work, locations, material, or product details; flag missing assets | Intentional abstract art or properly selected stock |
| Dashboard mockups on unrelated service sites | A fabricated interface substitutes for evidence | Show actual deliverables or a truthful explanation of the service | A software product with a real interface |
| Anonymous testimonial and logo walls | Proof is unverifiable or invented | Use supplied evidence with attribution and permission; omit unsupported claims | Verified customer evidence |
| Redundant sections and repeated facts | Repetition adds scroll without advancing understanding | Combine related content and give repeated CTAs meaningful placement | Helpful reminders along a long decision path |

## Execution and behavior

| Pattern to inspect | When it weakens the page | Specific repair | Legitimate use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Excessive gaps and oversized headings | Sparse content is stretched into a long page; useful evidence falls far below the opening | Adjust density, line lengths, and spacing to actual content | A deliberate image-led or contemplative experience |
| Scroll reveals, floating objects, cursor effects everywhere | Motion delays reading or overwhelms the action | Remove unneeded movement; tie motion to feedback or a focused visual moment | Purposeful storytelling with accessible alternatives |
| Hover lift on noninteractive cards | Implies an action that does not exist | Match visual feedback to actual interaction | A genuinely clickable object |
| Desktop layout simply stacked on mobile | Repeated boxes, poor crops, oversized gaps, and awkward heading breaks dominate | Review order, crop, density, and wrapping on narrow screens | Stacking that still supports the same task well |
| Muted text and translucent surfaces | Content or state becomes hard to distinguish | Measure relevant contrast; use dependable text and surface roles | Subtle decoration that does not carry essential information |
| Dead buttons and missing states | Visual polish masks an incomplete visitor path | Implement the promised action and appropriate pending, success, error, or empty state | Clearly disclosed nonfunctional concept work |

## How to prescribe a change

Weak: “This feels AI. Make it more premium.”

Useful: “The research findings and social links use the same rounded treatment, so the strongest evidence receives little emphasis. Give the study an open section with a title, scope, and direct link. Keep the primary contact button prominent and turn social links into quiet text links.”

Do not manufacture imperfections to suggest human authorship. Judge whether each choice works for this page.
